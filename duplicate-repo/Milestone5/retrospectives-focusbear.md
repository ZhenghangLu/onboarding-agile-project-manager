# Retrospective Documentation Summary (Focus Bear)

## Why Retrospectives Are Important

Sprint retrospectives at Focus Bear help our team reflect on successes, identify problems, and find ways to improve. Keeping good retrospective notes ensures we don't forget important issues and can see progress over time.

## How We Use Google Docs for Retrospectives

We document each retrospective clearly in Google Docs. For each session, we write down:
- **What went well** during the sprint.
- **What didn’t go well** or caused problems.
- **Ideas for improvement** (action items).

Having everything in Google Docs makes it easy to look back, track improvements, and hold ourselves accountable.

## Patterns We Found

From reviewing past retrospectives:
- A common theme is communication, specifically around task clarity and expectation setting.
- Technical challenges like bugs or unclear documentation regularly appear.
- Action items related to better planning and clearer responsibilities were frequent.

## Follow-Up on Action Items

We checked previous retrospectives and noticed:
- Many action items were completed, especially ones clearly assigned to specific team members.
- Some broader action items without clear owners were often overlooked.

## Challenges in Keeping Notes Actionable

The main challenge we saw is making sure action items are clear and specific. If items were vague or didn't have clear ownership, they rarely got done.

## How Retrospective Notes Help Long-term

Documenting retrospectives in Google Docs allows us to:
- Notice trends over several sprints.
- Identify issues that keep repeating.
- Track if our solutions are working or need adjustment.

## Steps for Better Retrospectives at Focus Bear

To ensure feedback turns into real improvements:
- Assign clear owners and deadlines for each action item.
- Regularly review previous retrospectives in planning meetings.
- Encourage the team to speak openly and honestly during retrospectives.

## Conclusion

At Focus Bear, using Google Docs to document retrospectives helps us continuously improve. Clear notes, specific action items, and regular follow-up are key to making meaningful progress as a team.
